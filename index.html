Repeated Applications of Simple Interest

Discover how interest grows when simple interest is applied repeatedly to an increasing amount.

Let's Learn Step by Step!

In this lesson, you will learn how interest is calculated repeatedly by applying simple interest to an updated amount each period.

Example 1: Let's Start!

Suppose you have a principal of ₱1,000 with an interest rate of 10% per period.

Let's calculate the interest earned in the first period.

Start with Period 1, then continue to Period 2 and Period 3. Click each period to explore its calculations.

Period 1

Simple Interest = Principal × Rate × Time

Interest = ₱1,000 × 0.10 × 1

Interest Earned = ₱100

New Amount = ₱1,000 + ₱100 = ₱1,100

Period 2

New Principal = ₱1,100

Simple Interest = Principal × Rate × Time

Interest = ₱1,100 × 0.10 × 1

Interest Earned = ₱110

New Amount = ₱1,100 + ₱110 = ₱1,210

Notice that the interest increased because the new principal is now ₱1,100 instead of ₱1,000.

Period 3

New Principal = ₱1,210

Simple Interest = Principal × Rate × Time

Interest = ₱1,210 × 0.10 × 1

Interest Earned = ₱121

New Amount = ₱1,210 + ₱121 = ₱1,331
